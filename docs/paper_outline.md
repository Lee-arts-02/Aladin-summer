# Paper Outline

Working title:

Human-AI Design Reasoning and Student Agency in GenAI-supported Sustainable Building Design

## Core Argument

The paper should not be framed only as a study of whether GenAI improves learning. The stronger contribution is that GenAI-CAD interaction produced different forms of designer agency depending on how students coupled their own design goals with AI-generated reasoning.

## Suggested Abstract Logic

1. GenAI-based CAD tools can support complex STEM design learning, but less is known about how students engage with AI reasoning during iterative design.
2. This study examined students' conceptual learning, designer profiles, and agency during a GenAI-supported sustainable building design task.
3. Data included pre/post assessments, design logs, AI reasoning logs, agency surveys, and open-ended/interview data.
4. Students demonstrated significant conceptual gains.
5. Human-AI trajectory analysis identified five designer profiles.
6. Agency analysis showed moderate agency overall, with self-adjustment and motivation strongest; log-survey integration suggested goal setting and self-cognition were associated with less off-task divergence.
7. ENA-style analysis showed that productive profiles had denser networks connecting goal-directed prompting, technical parameter use, AI energy reasoning, AI parameter recommendations, and uptake.
8. Findings suggest that effective GenAI-CAD learning depends on students' ability to monitor, select, and transform AI reasoning rather than simply receive AI output.

## Introduction Structure

1. GenAI and CAD are changing STEM/engineering design learning.
2. Sustainable building design is a rich context because it requires balancing conceptual understanding, engineering constraints, energy performance, and iterative design.
3. Existing GenAI education studies often focus on outcomes or prompt use, but less on the coupling between student reasoning and AI-generated reasoning.
4. Student agency is central: learners must set goals, monitor AI outputs, decide what to accept, and maintain ownership of design decisions.
5. This study contributes by integrating pre/post learning outcomes, human-AI design reasoning profiles, agency survey/open-ended data, and ENA-style network analysis.

## Research Questions

RQ1: Can interacting with a GenAI-based Computer-Aided Design (CAD) tool improve students' conceptual understanding of sustainable building design and STEM engineering education?

RQ2: Through this GenAI-guided CAD process, what different types of designers did students become, and how?

RQ3: How did interacting with a GenAI-based CAD tool on sustainable building design influence students' agency?

Use ANCOVA-style adjusted gain analysis and mixed model.

### RQ3 Analysis

### ENA-style Analysis

Describe ENA-style co-occurrence network analysis as an integrative analysis connecting RQ2 and RQ3.

## Results Structure

### RQ1 Results

Report descriptive pre/post scores, ANCOVA, mixed model, and figures.

Main result: significant conceptual gains after participation.

### RQ2 Results

Report model comparison and five designer profiles.

Main result: students became different designers depending on their human-AI reasoning coupling.

### RQ3 Results

Report survey agency means, reliability, exploratory log-survey correlations, open-ended themes, and joint display.

Main result: agency was strongest in self-adjustment and motivation; goal setting and self-cognition were associated with less off-task divergence.

### ENA Results

Report ENA projection, profile centroids, and contrast network.

Main result: AI-guided optimizers had stronger networks connecting goals, technical prompting, AI reasoning, and uptake.

## Discussion Structure

1. GenAI-CAD can support conceptual learning, but learning gains vary.
2. Productive GenAI use is not just frequent prompting; it is human-AI reasoning coupling.
3. Agency appears as selective and transformative uptake of AI reasoning.
4. Different designer profiles reveal different risks and opportunities:
   - AI-guided technical optimizers: productive integration.
   - Technical prompt composers: strong student intention but weaker AI reasoning uptake.
   - Visual-spatial generators: high surface generation but weaker engineering reasoning.
   - Off-task profiles: exploration can either coexist with technical iteration or become weakly coupled.
5. Design implications:
   - Prompt students to read and evaluate AI reasoning.
   - Scaffold goal setting and self-cognition.
   - Support transformative uptake rather than passive acceptance.
   - Use dashboards or reflective prompts to connect AI reasoning to design metrics.

## Limitations

- No randomized control group.
- Survey-log matching is limited because many survey responses lack names.
- Coding is currently rule-based and should be validated with human coders.
- ENA-style analysis is a co-occurrence approximation, not external ENA software output.
- Findings come from one GenAI-CAD environment and one sustainable building design task.

## Target Journal Fit

This paper can fit Computers & Education if framed around learning processes, human-AI reasoning, agency, and design learning rather than merely tool evaluation.
